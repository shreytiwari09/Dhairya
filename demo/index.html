The voice agent that lets you finish your number

Why Try it Numbers Details GitHub ↗

Voice agents often stop listening halfway through a number.

People pause while reading out a number. An agent that ends the turn after a fixed silence takes that pause as the end and records only half of it. Dhairya waits until the number is complete, then answers at once.

Built on Gnani Prisma v3.0 realtime speech-to-text and Gnani Timbre.

You pause. It takes that as the end.

People often read out a number in groups: five digits, a breath, five more.

Many voice agents decide you have finished after a fixed length of silence. If your pause is longer than their timer, they answer over you and record only half the number.

Make the timer longer and the agent stops cutting you off, but every other reply now takes longer too. Every fixed timer trades one harm for the other.

Why this problem.

It matters most where one wrong digit costs something: a callback number, a one-time code, an amount, a date. Five things led us here, each with its source.

  1. Gnani's own latency write-up calls fixed silence timers “the wrong tool”, and says this stage “most often ruins an otherwise fast system”.

    Gnani: what causes latency in voice AI

  2. Prisma's realtime API exposes silence settings and a manual commit frame; it has no setting that judges whether a value is complete.

    Gnani: realtime speech-to-text docs

  3. Telnyx ships a separate wait for transcripts that end in a number, so callers can finish reading out account IDs or phone numbers.

    Telnyx: interruption settings

  4. A Vapi support thread reports an agent interrupting a customer who is giving their details.

    Vapi support forum thread

  5. The leading open turn-detection model, Smart Turn, lists telephone-number and card-number entry as future work.

    Smart Turn on GitHub

        How it decides

        It counts the value, not the silence.

        1. ListenGnani Prisma v3.0 streams the call and returns text every time the caller pauses for 0.2 s.
        2. CountA parser reads the digits so far, in Hindi or English, in Devanagari or Latin script.
        3. DecideIncomplete: keep the line open, and ask the caller to go on after 4 s of nothing. Complete: answer at once.
        4. CheckThe whole turn is transcribed again through Prisma's REST endpoint, then read back with Gnani Timbre.
        What counts as complete
        ValueComplete when
        Mobile numberTen digits, starting 6 to 9
        OTP or PINThe expected four or six digits
        AmountA word like “रुपये” follows; otherwise a short grace (1.5 s after “हज़ार” or “लाख”)
        DateA day and a month, or a word like “कल”

        It understands digit by digit or in pairs, “double” and “triple”, lakh and crore, डेढ़ and ढाई, and English digits spoken inside Hindi.

        What we found in Gnani's APIThings the docs do not say
        • The realtime stream accepts exactly one language; a list such as hi-IN,en-IN is rejected. Hindi mode still hears English digits.
        • An undocumented header, x-partials, turns on partial transcripts. They lag by about a second, so Dhairya does not rely on them.
        • A 0.2 s silence window gave the right digits on 10 of 10 probe calls, with text about 0.4 s after the caller paused.
        • Three digits said together come back as one number: “eight nine five” arrives as “आठ सौ पंचानवे”. Found in the first live microphone test.
        • Timbre v3.0 voices read digit words with long gaps, which breaks recognition into single words; the test callers use v2.5 voices.
        LimitsWhat these results do not show
        • The callers are synthetic, made with Gnani Timbre, with pauses of 0.4 to 1.3 s. Real callers vary more.
        • Each breath group is spoken separately and joined with silence, so intonation is not natural. That is why the Smart Turn comparison is unfair to it.
        • Dhairya's figures are computed from recorded stream events. The live agent runs the same code in real time from a microphone.
        • The agent is told which kind of value it asked for. Amounts with no unit word still fall back on a short timer.
        • Only Hindi and English are measured so far.
        Built withModels, data and where it was built
        • Gnani Prisma v3.0, realtime and REST, hears every call. Gnani Timbre v2.5 speaks the test callers and the agent.
        • Smart Turn v3.2 by Pipecat, an open model, is the outside baseline and runs locally.
        • The benchmark: 200 synthetic calls. The first calls of each kind tuned one rule; the other 152 were recorded only after the rule was fixed.
        • Every caller, number, amount and date on this page is made up.
        • A solo build for Gnani AI's Great Indian AI Internship Challenge, from BML Munjal University, Kapriwas.
